## Finding 2 — measured, and it strengthens the `[W]` on necessity

The audit at `WP.tex:3288` reports *"`⊂` appears zero times across every entry"*, which reads as
impugning the five-mode claim. It does not: that census is over the **lexicon** (59 words + 20
closed-class categories). The paper says so — *"the algebra is closed over five by construction;
the lexicon witnesses four. Those are different statements"* — and the finding has been read as
the stronger one since.

**Measured across all `*.la`, 2026-09-09 — every mode is live in the build:**

| mode | glyph | uses | modules |
|---|---|---|---|
| metacursion | `⟲` MC | 125 | 31 |
| synthesis | `⊕` SYN | 116 | 33 |
| conjunction | `⊗` CON | 75 | 25 |
| direction | `▷` DIR | 66 | 26 |
| **containment** | **`⊂` CONT** | **43** | **21** |

`⊂` is additionally exercised as a *test predicate*, not merely used:
`canon_spec.la:318,325` — `CONT(MC(PRIM("DEPTH")))(SYN(PRIM("BEING"))(PRIM("FORM")))`.

**So the `[W]` on necessity holds at the build level.** The gap is real but narrower than
recorded: **no lexicon entry composes by containment.** That is a vocabulary gap, not an algebra
gap, and it should be stated as one.

## Finding 3 — the substantive ruling: is `[A]` on exactly-five *permanent*?

E11's second half asks this, and it is the only part still genuinely open. **It is two
obligations wearing one name, and they have opposite prognoses.**

### E11a — INDEPENDENCE (no two modes collapse into one): `[A]` today, `[W]` achievable now

The paper claims *"the build tests it by exhibiting constructions each mode alone makes
possible."* **Usage is not independence.** 43 uses of `⊂` show it is *used*, not that anything
becomes *unbuildable* without it.

**Buildable now, and cheap:** per mode, exhibit a structure constructed with it, then red-path by
removing that mode and showing the construction fails. Five red-paths, one per mode. That
converts independence from `[A]` to `[W]`.
⚠ The trap: a red-path that fails because the *symbol* is undefined witnesses nothing. It must
fail because the *structure* cannot be expressed by the remaining four.

### E11b — COMPLETENESS (no sixth is needed): **`[A]` permanently, as currently posed — because the claim is not well-formed**

"No sixth mode is needed" has no content until *needed for what* is answered **independently of
the five**. If "admissible structure" is defined as *what the five modes generate*, then
completeness is a tautology and proves nothing — it would be `Cl(M₀, five) = Cl(M₀, five)`.

**So E11b is not merely unproven; it is unprovable as stated.** A completeness proof requires a
prior, independent characterization of admissibility — a specification of what the language must
be able to express, written without reference to the mode set. **No such characterization exists
in the corpus.**

**Ruling: `[A]`, and stated as permanent *until an independent admissibility criterion is
written*.** That prerequisite is the real item, and it is not a proof task — it is a
specification task. Recording it as "a proof we haven't done yet" would misdescribe the work by
one whole category.
