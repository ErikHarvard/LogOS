# E12 — RULING: which derivation of the propositional connectives is the language's?

**Ruled 2026-09-09 by The Lieutenant at the General's instruction. Ratifiable or overrulable
by the General.** Blocks **M6 / W08** — the white paper's named next build.

---

## The question was malformed, and that is the finding

E12 was posed as *"two complete, incompatible derivations; one must be the language's."*
Reading both sources directly — not the summary reporting them — **three of the four apparent
conflicts do not exist**, and the one that does is settled by neither document.

### The two documents, read in full

| | White Paper `:2233–2247` | Codex II *(Logos & Paradox)* Table 4.1 `:1809–1821` |
|---|---|---|
| `∧` | composition | Identity applied to pairs |
| `∨` | determination — carving a boundary in Being | Excluded Middle applied to alternatives |
| `→` | **generation** — A's Being generates B's | **ρ at propositional resolution** — from A, recognize B |
| `¬` | not primitive; VOID composed with what is excluded; ¬ is *notation* | not derived from LNC; ¬ and LNC **co-derived** from `∃(∃) ≡ ∃`; ¬ is *notation* |
| `↔` | absent | Identity across propositions, **and `≡ (A→B) ∧ (B→A)`** |

### What dissolves

- **`¬` — the two agree exactly.** Both use the word *notation*. Both root the Law of
  Non-Contradiction in `∃(∃) ≡ ∃` directly rather than in `¬`. ⚠ **`ROADMAP-CURRENT.md` states
  E12 as "`¬` from Non-Contradiction". Codex II explicitly denies exactly that at `:1818`.**
  That mis-statement is what made the conflict look total. Corrected there.
- **`↔` — not a conflict.** Codex II itself writes `A ↔ B ≡ (A → B) ∧ (B → A)`: a *defined
  abbreviation*, not a primitive. The WP omits it from the **primitives**; Codex II includes it
  in the **inventory**. Two questions, two correct answers, one name. Bifurcated, it is nothing.
- **`∧`, `∨` — register difference, not disagreement.** "Composition"/"determination" are
  ontological readings; "Identity on pairs"/"EM on alternatives" are Law-mappings. Identical
  truth conditions. Both may stand.

### What survives: `→`, and the implementation settles it against BOTH

`metalogic.la:15` and `metalogic_spec.la:71`:

```
glyph IMPLIES = la a. la b. OR(NOT(a))(b)
```

`→` is a **defined abbreviation** for `¬A ∨ B`. It is not *generation* and it is not *ρ*.
Verified: **ρ is never wired to IMPLIES anywhere in the corpus**, and `IFF`/`BICOND` does not
exist in any module.

## THE RULING

**The language's propositional layer is: `¬`, `∧`, `∨` primitive · `→` defined as `¬A ∨ B` ·
`↔` defined as `(A→B) ∧ (B→A)`, currently unimplemented.**

1. Adopt **Codex II's inventory and its reducibility claims** — five connectives, two of them
   defined.
2. Adopt the **White Paper's primitive economy** — the primitive basis is smaller than the
   inventory, and that is not an omission.
3. **Strike "generation"** from WP `:2237` and **strike "ρ at propositional resolution"** from
   Codex II Table 4.1 item 4. Replace both with the definition that runs.
4. The ontological readings (composition / determination / exclusion) **stand as readings**.
   They are glosses on primitives, not competing derivations, and nothing forced them to compete.

**M6 / W08 is unblocked** on the four-primitive basis.

## ⚠ THE COST, STATED — and it is a second ruling, and it is the General's

This ruling makes a **stronger corpus claim currently unwitnessed**. Codex II Table 4.1 item 6:

> *"The truth tables are not stipulated — they are the Laws instantiated at propositional
> resolution."*

The implementation says otherwise. In `metalogic.la` the three laws operate on **terms** and
**consume** the connectives:

```
glyph LAW_IDENTITY         = la g.    AUTO_OK(g)
glyph LAW_NONCONTRADICTION = la term. INHABITS(DECL_ARITY(term))(BODY_ARITY(term))
glyph LAW_EXCLUDED_MIDDLE  = la term. WELLFORMED(term)
glyph NC_TYPECHECK = la dec. la bod. NOT(AND(INHABITS(dec)(bod))(NOT(INHABITS(dec)(bod))))
```

`canon.la:33,35` does the same: `LAW_NC` and `LAW_EM` are **written using** `NOT`/`AND`/`OR`.
**The dependency runs laws ← connectives, the reverse of what both documents claim.** And
`NOT = la b. b(FALSE)(TRUE)` is a stipulated Church boolean, derived from nothing.

**So: either something must derive the Church encodings from `∃(∃) ≡ ∃`, or that claim drops
from `[W]` to `[A]`.** It cannot stay `[W]` on the present code. That call is the General's.

## FALSIFIERS — what would overturn this

1. **A module that derives a connective FROM a law** rather than using it. Searched all `*.la`;
   found none. Anyone who finds one collapses item 3 of the ruling.
2. **A `ρ`-wired implication** anywhere in the corpus. Searched; none.
3. If `ρ`'s definition elsewhere turns out to entail `¬A ∨ B`, then Codex II item 4 was right
   all along and only the WP's "generation" should be struck.

## ADJACENT FINDING — not part of this ruling, filed separately

`NOT` is redefined in **~80 modules** with at least **five structurally distinct forms**:
`la b. b(FALSE)(TRUE)` · `la p. la t. la f. p(f)(t)` · `la b. la t. la f. b(f)(t)` ·
`la a. a(FALSE)(TRUE)` · `la c. c(FALSE)(TRUE)`. `AND` has two, `OR` has two.
They appear extensionally equivalent. **But the White Paper's own monosemy claim is that
`κ(g)` IS simultaneously the word's shape and the word's sense** — under which one name with
five shapes is a monosemy violation in the corpus's own terms, and no gate checks it.
